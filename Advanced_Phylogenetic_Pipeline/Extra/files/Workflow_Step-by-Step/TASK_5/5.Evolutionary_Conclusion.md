# Evolutionary Conclusion: Glycine max (Soybean)

## 1. Overview
*Glycine max* (soybean) is a polyploid crop species with a complex genome history due to multiple whole-genome duplication (WGD) events. Our analysis identified a significant expansion of MIKCc-type MADS-box genes, totaling 87 candidate genes.

## 2. Clade Distribution & Identification
The circular phylogenetic trees (MAFFT, MUSCLE, ClustalO alignments with NJ and aBayes) show substantial gene expansion across almost all MIKCc subclades.

*   **AP1/FUL Clade:** Extensive expansion of AP1/FUL-like genes, with multiple orthologs clustering around AT1G69120 and AT5G60910, reflecting its complex flowering and seed production pathways.
*   **AG/STK Clade:** Multiple paralogs related to AG (AT3G58780), showing redundant yet distinct functions in fruit development.
*   **SEP Clade:** Significant expansion of SEP-like genes compared to *Arabidopsis*, consistent with the multiple rounds of WGD and the development of legume-specific floral structures.
*   **SOC1 Clade:** High diversity in SOC1-like genes, reflecting the integration of multiple cues for flowering time control in soybean.

## 3. Evolutionary Inference
The phylogeny highlights the massive expansion of the MADS-box family in *Glycine max* due to the soybean-specific WGD event (~13 million years ago) and an earlier papilionoid WGD (~59 million years ago). This redundancy has likely enabled extensive sub-functionalization and neo-functionalization, contributing to the diversity of legume floral and fruit traits.

## 4. Methodological Consistency
High support values (1000 replicates) in both NJ and aBayes trees across multiple alignments confirm that these numerous paralogs are correctly assigned to their respective ancestral MIKCc lineages.

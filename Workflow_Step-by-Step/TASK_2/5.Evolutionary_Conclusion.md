# Evolutionary Conclusion: Amborella trichopoda

## 1. Overview
*Amborella trichopoda*, being the sister group to all other extant flowering plants, represents a crucial ancestral state in the evolution of MADS-box genes. Our analysis identified 16 candidate MIKCc-type MADS-box genes, reflecting the pre-duplication (WGD) ancestral repertoire.

## 2. Clade Distribution & Identification
The circular phylogenetic trees (MAFFT, MUSCLE, ClustalO alignments combined with NJ and aBayes) show a simplified yet clear distribution of these genes across the major MIKCc subclades.

*   **Ancestral Lineages:** Most *Amborella* genes appear as sister sequences to the more expanded lineages in *Arabidopsis* and other eudicots/monocots.
*   **AP1/FUL Clade:** *Amborella* possesses foundational orthologs of AP1/FUL, ancestral to floral meristem identity regulators.
*   **AG/STK Clade:** Conserved orthologs of AG (AT3G58780) and STK (AT4G18960) are present, suggesting the floral homeotic (C and D function) machinery was already well-defined in early angiosperms.
*   **SEP Clade:** A limited set of SEPALLATA-like genes is observed, consistent with their role in floral organ identity across all angiosperms.

## 3. Evolutionary Inference
The relatively low number of genes (16) compared to *Glycine max* or *Oryza sativa* confirms that *Amborella* has not undergone the multiple whole-genome duplication (WGD) events seen in later-diverging lineages. Its MADS-box family provides a high-resolution snapshot of the ancestral floral toolkit before the radiation of core eudicots.

## 4. Methodological Consistency
All three alignment strategies (MAFFT, MUSCLE, ClustalO) correctly resolved the basal position of *Amborella* sequences in the combined phylogeny, with high (1000) bootstrap support for the major clade nodes.

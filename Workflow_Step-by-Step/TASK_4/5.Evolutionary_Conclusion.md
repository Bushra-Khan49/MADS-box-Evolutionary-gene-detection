# Evolutionary Conclusion: Cinnamomum kanehirae

## 1. Overview
*Cinnamomum kanehirae* (stout camphor tree) is a magnoliid, representing one of the major lineages of Mesangiospermae. Its 31 identified MIKCc-type MADS-box genes offer important insights into the evolution of wood and floral development in magnoliids.

## 2. Clade Distribution & Identification
The circular phylogenetic trees (MAFFT, MUSCLE, ClustalO alignments with NJ and aBayes) show the existence of well-defined clades across the 12 MIKCc major lineages.

*   **AP1/FUL Clade:** Conserved clustering of *Cinnamomum* orthologs with AT1G69120 and AT5G60910, reflecting a role in floral meristem identity.
*   **AG/STK Clade:** Stably clustered with AT3G58780 (AG), reinforcing its role in reproductive organ identity.
*   **SEP Clade:** Moderate expansion of SEPALLATA-like genes, characteristic of magnoliid genomes.

## 3. Evolutionary Inference
The phylogeny confirms the phylogenetic position of *Cinnamomum kanehirae* as a magnoliid, sister to the eudicot/monocot clade. The diversification of its MADS-box family illustrates the early evolutionary expansion of the floral developmental toolkit in Mesangiospermae.

## 4. Methodological Consistency
High support values (1000 replicates) in both NJ and aBayes trees across multiple alignments confirm the robustness of its phylogenetic placement.
